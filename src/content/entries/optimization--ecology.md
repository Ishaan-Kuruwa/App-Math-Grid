title: "How many fish to catch, and which land to protect"
method: optimization
field: ecology
status: reviewed
difficulty: intermediate
summary: >-
  Conservation means making the best choices with limited resources. In fisheries,
  the question is how many fish can be caught each year without shrinking the
  population over time; the largest such catch is called the maximum sustainable
  yield, and many countries are legally required to manage fish stocks around it.
  On land, the question is which areas to protect when there is only enough money
  for some of them. Optimization turns both into math problems: pick the catch
  level or the set of protected areas that does the most good while staying within
  limits. Researchers have shown that the best catch for one fish species depends on
  how much of the others is caught, and have chosen reserves that give wolves,
  bears, and other species the best chance of surviving for the long term.
starterQuestion: >-
  A fish population grows by r·P·(1 − P/K) each year, where P is the population, K
  is the most the lake can support, and r is the growth rate. If you catch a fixed
  number of fish every year, what is the largest catch that keeps the population
  steady? Try it for K = 10,000 and r = 0.4, then simulate what happens if you catch
  10% more than that.
prerequisites:
  - "Algebra, including finding the maximum of a quadratic"
  - "Growth rates and percent change"
  - "A spreadsheet or basic coding"
