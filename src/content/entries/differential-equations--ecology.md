title: "Predators, prey, and the equations that link them"
method: differential-equations
field: ecology
status: reviewed
difficulty: intro
summary: >-
  Population models describe how fast a population changes. In logistic growth, a
  population grows quickly at first and then levels off as it nears the most its
  habitat can support. The Lotka–Volterra model links two equations, one for a
  predator and one for its prey: prey grow on their own but get eaten, and
  predators grow when they eat and die off when food runs short. Because each
  population's rate of change depends on the other, the model produces
  boom-and-bust cycles: more prey leads to more predators, which leads to fewer
  prey, and then fewer predators. You can explore both models in a spreadsheet by
  stepping forward in small time steps, and ecologists fit the same equations to
  feeding experiments to measure how fast a predator really eats.
starterQuestion: >-
  Using a Lotka–Volterra model of rabbits and foxes, what happens to both
  populations over 50 years if a disease raises the foxes' death rate by 20%?
  Simulate the model in a spreadsheet using small time steps and compare the
  cycles before and after.
