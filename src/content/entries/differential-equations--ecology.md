title: "Predators, prey, and the equations that link them"
method: differential-equations
field: ecology
status: draft
difficulty: intermediate
summary: >-
  The Lotka–Volterra model uses two linked differential equations to describe a
  predator and its prey. Prey grow on their own but get eaten; predators grow when
  they eat and die off when food runs short. Because each population's rate of
  change depends on the other, the model produces boom-and-bust cycles: more prey
  leads to more predators, which leads to fewer prey, and then fewer predators.
  Ecologists change the equations to ask "what if?" questions, such as what happens
  when predators also eat each other, and they fit the equations to feeding
  experiments to measure how fast a predator really eats.
starterQuestion: >-
  Using a Lotka–Volterra model of rabbits and foxes, what happens to both
  populations over 50 years if a disease raises the foxes' death rate by 20%?
  Simulate the model in a spreadsheet using small time steps and compare the
  cycles before and after.
