title: "Cheapest healthy diets, smartest investments, and farm planning"
method: optimization
field: economics
status: reviewed
difficulty: intermediate
summary: >-
  Economics is about making the best choice when money, time, or resources are
  limited, and optimization is the math that finds that best choice. The classic
  "diet problem" asks for the cheapest set of foods that still meets every
  nutritional need; it is solved with linear programming, which handles a goal and
  a list of limits that are all straight-line (linear) relationships. Investors use
  a related idea from Harry Markowitz: choose a mix of investments that gives the
  highest expected return for the amount of risk you can accept. Researchers have
  used these tools to design affordable, healthy, and low-carbon diets, to plan
  savings for separate goals such as retirement and college, and to model how a
  farm family in Tonga decides what to grow and what to eat.
starterQuestion: >-
  Pick 5 foods from a grocery store website and look up their prices and nutrition
  labels. What is the cheapest combination that gives at least 2,000 calories, 50 g
  of protein, and 25 g of fiber in a day? Solve it with a spreadsheet solver, then
  add a rule of your own (for example, at most 2 servings of any food) and see how
  the cost changes.
prerequisites:
  - "Algebra and graphing linear inequalities"
  - "Percentages and averages"
  - "A spreadsheet with a solver tool, or basic coding"
