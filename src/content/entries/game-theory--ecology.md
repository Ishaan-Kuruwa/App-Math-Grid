title: "Fight or back off? Game theory in animal behavior"
method: game-theory
field: ecology
status: reviewed
difficulty: intermediate
summary: >-
  When two animals compete for food or territory, each one's best move depends on
  what the other does. The Hawk–Dove game models this: a "hawk" always fights, a
  "dove" backs off, and the payoffs depend on how valuable the prize is and how
  costly a lost fight is. Over many generations, the population settles into a
  stable mix of the two behaviors, which you can find with algebra. The same
  reasoning explains why animals that arrived first usually keep their territory,
  how group size changes conflict, and why fishing fleets that share the same fish
  tend to overfish.
starterQuestion: >-
  In the Hawk–Dove game, how does the stable share of aggressive animals change as
  the cost of losing a fight rises compared with the value of the prize? Work out
  the formula, then check it by simulating a population that changes over many
  generations.
