title: "How drug levels rise and fall in the body"
method: differential-equations
field: medicine
status: draft
difficulty: intro
summary: >-
  Pharmacokinetics is the study of how a drug moves through the body after a dose.
  The simplest model treats the body as one container: the rate at which the drug
  leaves is proportional to how much is there, which gives a differential equation
  whose solution is exponential decay. From it you get a drug's half-life and can
  see why repeated doses build up to a steady level. More detailed models link
  several containers, one for each organ, joined by blood flow. The same ideas
  describe harmful substances too, such as how lead is absorbed and stored in the
  body.
starterQuestion: >-
  Suppose a patient takes a 200 mg dose of a drug with a 6-hour half-life every 8
  hours. Using a one-compartment model, how high does the amount of drug in the body
  climb, and after how many doses does it level off?
