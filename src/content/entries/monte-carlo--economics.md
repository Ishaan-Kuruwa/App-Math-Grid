title: "Will the money last? Simulating savings and investments"
method: monte-carlo
field: economics
status: reviewed
difficulty: intro
summary: >-
  Nobody knows what the stock market will do next year, so nobody can say exactly
  how much a savings account will be worth in 30 years. Monte Carlo simulation
  answers a different question: across thousands of possible futures, each with
  investment returns picked at random, how often does the plan work? Researchers
  have used this to study retirement. One study found that people generally need
  to save at least 15% of their salary for 30 or more years, with at least half
  invested in stocks, to have a high chance of their money lasting. Another showed
  that retirees who adjust how much they withdraw each year, based on how their
  investments are doing, run out of money far less often than those who withdraw
  a fixed amount. Banks use the same idea to estimate the fair price of financial
  contracts whose value depends on future prices.
starterQuestion: >-
  Someone saves $5,000 a year for 30 years. Each year their savings grow by a
  random return: 7% on average, but anywhere from −20% to +30%. Simulate 10,000
  possible 30-year futures. What is the chance they end with more than $500,000?
  How much does that chance change if they save $6,000 a year instead, or if they
  start five years later?
