title: "Where to put defibrillators, ambulances, and bus stops"
method: optimization
field: public-policy
status: reviewed
difficulty: intro
summary: >-
  Cities have limited money for services like ambulances, defibrillators, and school
  buses, so where they put them matters. Optimization turns this into a math
  problem: choose the locations or routes that cover the most people, or get
  everyone served at the lowest cost, while staying within limits like budget,
  travel time, or bus capacity. Researchers have used real records of where cardiac
  arrests happen to place public defibrillators, built models that keep ambulances
  within a few minutes of most residents, and planned school bus routes that pick
  up every student on time.
starterQuestion: >-
  Using a map of your town or school campus, where should 3 defibrillators go so
  that the most people are within a 2-minute walk of one? Compare a "pick the best
  spot, then the next best" method with checking every possible combination.
